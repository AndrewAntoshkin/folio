From ambiguity to production.

I join when a task is not ready to hand to engineering. First I work out what has to be done, and by which rules. Then I build something the decision can be tested on. I stay until it reaches people and the team can carry it without me.

This is how I work on the design system at Yandex HR Tech now. Before that, design systems at Sber, and products at Otkritie Broker and MTS.

How the work goes

(4)
01

Context

The problem, the scenarios, the constraints, and how we will know it worked. Until that can be said, a mockup only hides the ambiguity.

02

Prototype

Not the whole product. The scenarios where the decision could fall apart. Figma or code, whichever gets something real to argue about sooner. The argument should be about the thing, not about an opinion.

03

Rules

States, components, tokens. One specification for design, engineering, and an agent if it will assemble the interface later. Otherwise everyone builds their own version.

04

To people

A mockup is not the end of the work for me. I stay for the implementation, the checks, and the way the team will change it later without losing quality.

With an agent

An agent takes the sequence: find what the system already has, draft, run the checks. I set the task, set the boundaries, and look at the result.

I don’t use one long prompt. In it, the agent is author and reviewer of its own work. A step has an input, an output, and a moment where I say whether it is done.

The product decision, the tradeoff between quality and time, and anything that goes out under my name stay with me.

A good process does not promise the model will never be wrong. It makes the error visible and stops it from passing unnoticed.

More on this is in the essay on how AI changes processes.
